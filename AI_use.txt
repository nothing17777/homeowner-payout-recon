AI USE
 
Tool: Claude Code (Claude Sonnet 5.5)
 
What I used it for
- Unpacking the project and reading the brief and the three CSVs.
- Scaffolding the project and drafting the loader, payout rules, reconciliation
  matcher, CLI, unit tests, and README.
 
What I did myself
- Worked out the expected deposit for each reservation by hand from the brief's
  rules, independently of the code, and used that as the reference to check the
  matcher's output against the bank feed.
- Derived and verified the GuestyPay fee formula. The brief gives 2.9% + $0.30
  but not the rounding order or whether taxes are in the base. I picked one
  reading and confirmed it reproduces all four GuestyPay settlements in the bank
  file to the cent. A different rounding order is off by a penny on at least one.
- Wrote the unit test that exposed the refund-netting bug below, and reviewed and
  corrected the fix.
- Decided the twelve assumptions in the README. Where the brief is ambiguous, the
  choice of what to assume and what to flag is mine, not the model's.
- Reviewed the plan before implementation and ran the test suite.
 
Example where the AI got something wrong, and how I caught it
The first version of the matcher matched a refunded Airbnb reservation's original
deposit on its own and never looked for the negative refund adjustment that
followed it. The adjustment was left over as an orphan "unmatched refund", and the
reservation was reported as fully reconciled even though the bank had clawed back
part of it. The real data did not expose this: R1014's two legs are each off by
4.65, so it took a different code path. I wrote a unit test for the clean case
(deposit 240, later adjustment -50 on a reservation refunded 50) and it failed
with status "reconciled" instead of "reconciled (refund netted)". The fix attaches
the adjustment to the existing match (step 1b in payout_recon/reconcile.py).
 
A second, smaller one: the first draft of the exceptions list left out matches that
only reconciled within a tolerance (R1018, off by $0.02) or net of a refund (R1014).
The brief asks for "anything that doesn't reconcile", so I added them as
`matched_with_variance`.
