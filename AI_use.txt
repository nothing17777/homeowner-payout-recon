Example where the AI got something wrong, and how it was caught
The first version of the matcher matched a refunded Airbnb reservation's original deposit
on its own and never looked for the negative refund adjustment that followed it. The
adjustment was left over as an orphan "unmatched refund", and the reservation was shown as
fully reconciled even though the bank had clawed back part of it. The real data did not
expose this: R1014's two legs are each off by 4.65, so it took a different code path. A
unit test I wrote for the clean case (deposit 240, later adjustment -50 on a reservation
refunded 50) failed with status "reconciled" instead of "reconciled (refund netted)".
The fix attaches the adjustment to the existing match (step 1b in payout_recon/reconcile.py).

A second, smaller one: the first draft of the exceptions list left out matches that only
reconciled within a tolerance (R1018, off by $0.02) or net of a refund (R1014). The brief
asks for "anything that doesn't reconcile", so I added them as `matched_with_variance`.
